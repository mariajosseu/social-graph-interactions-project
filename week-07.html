Limitation. An embedding captures patterns learned from other text, not a ground-truth measure of fictional relatedness. A missing hyperlink is not evidence that two characters are unrelated, and a nearby dot is not proof that two pages mean the same thing.

Method / The instrumentDOCUMENT EMBEDDING × COSINE

Method / How the measurement is made

Before we trust the dots.

A pretrained encoder gives each page a dense vector. Long pages are split into token-limited chunks, each chunk is embedded, and the chunk vectors are mean-pooled into one representation per page. Cosine similarity is calculated in that original space; a 2D map is only a drawing of it.

How the three instruments were built
01 / Fixed lookup

A deliberately small lexicon

Every matched positive word adds 1; every matched negative word subtracts 1. Zero means neutral for this instrument. No rules for negation or word order. The list deliberately includes “doom”, “sinister” and “carnage”, so name sensitivity here is built in, not a discovery about all lexicons.

02 / Learned word weights

TF-IDF + logistic regression

03 / Contextual classifier

DistilBERT / SST-2

The course’s pretrained binary classifier, fine-tuned on movie-review sentiment. Each sentence is scored independently with a 512-token cap. Neither learned classifier was fitted to these Marvel tests.

Binary classifiers must pick positive or negative even for mixed language. We leave those cases marked mixed in the intended readings, rather than treating one forced label as the right answer. Sarcasm also depends on tone and context.

Negativity is not an emotion.

DistilBERT’s negative label can reflect many kinds of language. DeepMoji predicts emoji; here, anger is the combined probability of 😡 and 😠. Neither instrument directly measures a character’s feelings.

The slider reads the smoothed curves. The three excerpts are the highest-scoring sentences within the 5% negativity, anger and relative-mismatch windows; their scores refer to the individual sentence. Longer sentences are excerpted. Follow the source link to read the surrounding story.

What the experiment found

Two things stand out.

How we did it

The stories come from the public FanFiction.net archive. Each story’s title, author and source link appear above its curves.

01 / Score

Two different signals

We score each sentence twice: DistilBERT estimates negative sentiment, while DeepMoji predicts emoji. We add its probabilities for 😡 and 😠 as a rough anger signal.

02 / Align

Beginning to end

We average nearby sentences to reveal broader changes, using a window of 3%, 5% or 10% of the story. We then map every story onto 100 points from beginning to end, so different lengths can be compared.

03 / Cluster

Let six compete

We compare 2–8 groups of similarly shaped curves, then see which grouping separates them best. The six familiar shapes help describe the results; several groups can resemble the same shape.
